US Used Car Market Warehouse

Salvage titles sell for 87% less: $2,425 versus an $18,000 median. The warehouse is a star schema of 2,499 auction listings and 41,804 EPA records, with every removed row logged.

  • 2,413 clean listings from 2,499
  • 86.5% lower median on a salvage title
  • 99.96% matched to EPA make and year
  • r = −0.33 price versus mileage

Two columns were left out of the analysis. condition is auction time left, such as “2 days left” (832 rows) and “21 hours left” (492 rows). model is unreliable: its most common values are “door” (651), “doors” (148) and “mpv” (87). The EPA join uses make and model year.

Findings

Title status is the biggest price gap

A salvage title cuts the median from $18,000 to $2,425, a drop of 86.5%. That is a wider gap than any mileage band or model year. Clean titles are 2,318 listings. Salvage titles are 95.

Price falls with mileage, steepest in the first 50,000 miles

On clean titles, the first extra 25,000 miles take about $8,200, or 33%, off the median. After 100,000 miles the price flattens. Across individual listings the correlation is r = −0.33.

Depreciation by age

On clean titles, the steep drops are year 2 (−27.6%) and years 6 and 7 (−27.3% and −34.4%). Age 0 is cheaper than age 1, and only 47 cars are 2020 models, so that bump is noise.

The Midwest premium is a mix of newer, lower-mileage cars

Midwest listings average $23,147, about $5,400 above the South. Those Midwest cars average 36,000 miles and 2.5 years old. Texas, the cheapest state in the file at $14,460, averages 68,000 miles and 3.8 years. The gap is which cars are listed where.

Nissan is young and cheap

Among brands with 30 or more clean listings, Nissan cars are the youngest and least driven, and still about $10,000 below Ford. A brand’s EPA lineup fuel economy has only a weak link to price (r = −0.15). Price tracks mileage (r = −0.33) and model year (r = +0.23) more closely.

Limits

  • Half the listings are Ford (50%), so brand and regional averages lean toward Ford.
  • These are auction listings, not dealer retail prices. Many are insurance and fleet disposals.
  • The model field is unreliable, so results stay at the brand level.
  • The regional gaps are not adjusted for mileage, age or brand. A regression would be the next step.

What the SQL demonstrates

  • Staging tables, cleaning rules in transform.sql, and a star schema, with every removed row logged.
  • A median from ROW_NUMBER() and COUNT() OVER, because MySQL has no PERCENTILE_CONT.
  • Year-over-year change with LAG(), rank within region with RANK(), and share of total with SUM() OVER ().
  • Pearson correlation from AVG and STDDEV_POP.
  • A join of two public datasets on normalized keys, including lowercase make and “land” to Land Rover.
