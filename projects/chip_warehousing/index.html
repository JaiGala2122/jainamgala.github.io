Chip Warehousing and Shipping Optimization

A seven-day plan for 9,215 orders. Modeled warehouse cost fell by $141,691.76, and no warehouse broke its daily capacity.

  • 9,215 orders assigned, zero daily capacity violations
  • $141,692 lower modeled warehouse cost, about 0.9%
  • 1,611 orders moved to a different warehouse
  • 3.66s final solver time. Exact optimality was not proved

Project overview

The question was practical: can the current network handle these orders, and is there a cheaper assignment that still works? Excel and Power Query checked examples. Python repeated those checks across the full dataset. A mixed-integer linear program chose the warehouse, the shipping route, and the processing day together.

This is a public portfolio case built on the Brunel Supply Chain Logistics Problem Dataset. It is not a client engagement.

Dataset and tools

Scope. 9,215 orders, 19 warehouses, and 1,540 freight-rate records. Seven tables: OrderList, FreightRates, PlantPorts, ProductsPerPlant, VmiCustomers, WhCapacities, and WhCosts.

Tools. Excel, Power Query, Python, pandas, NumPy, openpyxl, SciPy, and HiGHS (highspy).

Assumptions and limits

Seven days is a planning scenario. All orders are available at the start. Processing and dispatch share a day. No delivery deadlines or delay penalties are modeled.

Whole orders must satisfy product, customer, port, service, and daily order-capacity rules. Warehouse cost is quantity times the unit rate, not a daily holding cost.

Compatible orders on the same route and day share a weight band. AIR charges apply per order. GROUND charges apply per group. Customer-arranged freight costs the company nothing in freight, and the warehouse cost still applies.

The 48-slot PLANT03 cushion is arithmetic. A capacity-loss stress test has not been completed.
